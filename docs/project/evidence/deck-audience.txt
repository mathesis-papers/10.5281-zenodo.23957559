 One row from its two neighbours

          For real m and integers 1 ≤ k ≤ n,

                                 Tm (n, 1) = Tm (n, n) = 1.

          For 1 < k < n only,

                         Tm (n, k) = [m(n − k) + 1]Tm (n − 1, k − 1)
                                         + [m(k − 1) + 1]Tm (n − 1, k).

          At m = 0: Pascal’s triangle. For negative m, the weights can be
          negative.

          Question
          Can every even row still be strictly positive?

 The sign pattern holds for real parameters




          Theorem 2.1 (Paper Theorem 1 (thm:main))                WRITTEN PROOF

          For every real r ≥ 3, at step m = −r:
              T−r (n, k) > 0 for positive even n, 1 ≤ k ≤ n;
              T−r (n, k) < 0 for odd n ≥ 3, 1 < k < n.
          Thus the three OEIS conjectures and Chen–Chen Conjecture 2
          hold for their stated integer parameters.

 The boundary needs its own lemma



          Lemma 3.1 (Paper Lemma 2 (lem:second))                 WRITTEN PROOF

          For real r ≥ 3, T−r (n, 2) > 0 whenever n ≥ 2 is even.

          Put an = T−r (n, 2). Eliminating one row gives

                         an+2 = (r − 1)2 an + r(r − 2)n + 2 − r2 .

          The first two even values are a2 = 1 and

                                 a4 = 2r(r − 3) + 3 ≥ 3.

          For n ≥ 4, the forcing term is strictly positive.

 Two rows close the induction



            1   Start with row 2 = (1, 1).
            2   Positive even row n plus two negative weights gives a negative
                odd interior in row n + 1.
            3   In row n + 2, two negative predecessors and two negative
                weights give a positive interior.
            4   Boundaries remain 1. Lemma 2 and reflection handle the two
                positions next to them.

          The range 3 ≤ k ≤ n is empty when n = 2; the boundary argument
          still covers the whole next row.

 Exactly which integer steps work?



          Corollary 3.2 (Paper Corollary 3 (cor:classification))
          WRITTEN PROOF
          For integer m, every positive even row is entrywise nonnegative iff

                                 m ≤ −3     or m ≥ 0.

          For exactly these parameters all positive even rows are strictly
          positive.

          For m ≥ 0 both weights are positive. The remaining integers fail at

            Tm (4, 2) = Tm (4, 3) = 2m2 + 6m + 3 = −1                     (m = −1, −2).

 Positive coeﬀicients give a root annulus



          Corollary 4.2 (Paper Corollary 5 (cor:annulus))                 WRITTEN PROOF

          For real m ≤ −3, even n ≥ 2, every root z of Rm,n satisfies

                                      Ln ≤ |z| ≤ Un ,

          where Ln and Un are the minimum and maximum of
          Tm (n, k)/Tm (n, k + 1), 1 ≤ k < n. All ratios are positive and
          Ln Un = 1.

          Eneström–Kakeya: rescale coeﬀicients to be monotone, then use
          the triangle inequality. See Kakeya (1912), reference 7 in the book.

 Oddness and congruences prevent zero



          Let Aq (m) = Rm,2q+1 (−1), with A0 (m) = 1.

          Corollary 5.1 (Paper Corollary 6 (cor:odd))          WRITTEN PROOF

          For every integer q ≥ 1,

                    Aq (−4)/8q is odd,      Aq (−5) ≡ 1 (mod 3).

          The first holds with (−4, 8) replaced by (−r, 2r) for every positive
          integer 4 | r; the second holds for every integer m ≡ 1 (mod 3).
          Both source assertions therefore hold for all odd rows.

 The excluded steps have unit-modulus roots



          Corollary 6.2 (Paper Corollary 8 (cor:boundaryroots))
          WRITTEN PROOF
          For q ≥ 1,

                         R−1,2q = R−2,2q = (1 − y)2q−2 (1 + y),
                                R−1,2q+1 = (1 − y)2q−2 (1 + y2 ),
                                R−2,2q+1 = (1 − y)2q .

          Thus Mn (−1) = Mn (−2) = 1 for n ≥ 2; both growth exponents
          are zero. Also R0,n = (1 + y)n−1 .

 Positive coeﬀicients need not mean real roots



          Corollary 6.2 (Paper Corollary 8 (cor:boundaryroots))
          WRITTEN PROOF
          For real m > 0 and n ≥ 2, all roots are simple and negative. At
          m = −3, nonreal roots occur already in row 6.


                  R−3,6 (y) = (y + 1)(y4 + 16y3 + 246y2 + 16y + 1).

 What this manuscript does not establish




              All-row reality of the roots at steps −4, −5 is not covered.
              Remaining complex-parameter asymptotics are not covered.
              A fixed positive inner radius cannot contain all scaled zeros:
              the even-row root −1 scales toward zero.
              Finite plots and reported tightness percentages are separate
              numerical claims.

          The finite-row annulus remains valid. The growth theorem
          concerns the largest modulus, not every scaled root.
