 One row from its two neighbours

          For real m and integers 1 ≤ k ≤ n,

                                 Tm (n, 1) = Tm (n, n) = 1.

          For 1 < k < n only,

                         Tm (n, k) = [m(n − k) + 1]Tm (n − 1, k − 1)
                                         + [m(k − 1) + 1]Tm (n − 1, k).

          At m = 0: Pascal’s triangle. For negative m, the weights can be
          negative.

          Question
          Can every even row still be strictly positive?

 The sign pattern holds for real parameters




          Theorem 2.1 (Paper Theorem 1 (thm:main))                WRITTEN PROOF

          For every real r ≥ 3, at step m = −r:
              T−r (n, k) > 0 for positive even n, 1 ≤ k ≤ n;
              T−r (n, k) < 0 for odd n ≥ 3, 1 < k < n.
          Thus the three OEIS conjectures and Chen–Chen Conjecture 2
          hold for their stated integer parameters.

 The boundary needs its own lemma



          Lemma 3.1 (Paper Lemma 2 (lem:second))                 WRITTEN PROOF

          For real r ≥ 3, T−r (n, 2) > 0 whenever n ≥ 2 is even.

          Put an = T−r (n, 2). Eliminating one row gives

                         an+2 = (r − 1)2 an + r(r − 2)n + 2 − r2 .

          The first two even values are a2 = 1 and

                                 a4 = 2r(r − 3) + 3 ≥ 3.

          For n ≥ 4, the forcing term is strictly positive.

 What this manuscript does not establish




              All-row reality of the roots at steps −4, −5 is not covered.
              Remaining complex-parameter asymptotics are not covered.
              A fixed positive inner radius cannot contain all scaled zeros:
              the even-row root −1 scales toward zero.
              Finite plots and reported tightness percentages are separate
              numerical claims.

          The finite-row annulus remains valid. The growth theorem
          concerns the largest modulus, not every scaled root.
